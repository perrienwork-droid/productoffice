Four ways to make the formula earn its placement.

The current version is five identical text cards. Each option below replaces it with something that gives the eye something to do, while staying in the page's editorial register (Fraunces, warm palette, restrained).

Option A

The annotated diagram.

A single illustrated pair of sunglasses, with the five criteria as numbered callouts. Most magazine-y, gives the section a "hero artefact", and the criteria become a glossary for the diagram. Strongest visual punch.

i
Warm frame

Tortoise, champagne, milky pink, cream.

ii
Soft cat-eye

Subtle upsweep, lifts the face.

iii
Medium-large

Generous but not oversized.

iv
Warm lens tint

Brown, amber, honey gradient.

v
Gold accents

Matches jewellery and palette.

Option B

Each criterion gets a swatch.

Keeps the five-column rhythm you already have, but each card opens with a concrete visual artefact (a colour chip, a shape, a scale, a gradient, a gold dot). Lowest-risk upgrade, slots in without changing the layout.

i.
Warm frame

Tortoise, champagne, milky pink, cream. Cool whites and greys clash with warm undertones.

ii.
Soft cat-eye

Or squared cat-eye with subtle upsweep. Lifts the face. Pure rounds and sharp angles underperform.

iii.
Medium-large

Generous but not oversized. Small narrow frames ageing, oversized round overwhelming.

iv.
Warm lens tint

Brown, amber, honey gradient make brown eyes glow. Cool blue/pink clashes.

v.
Gold accents

Matches existing jewellery and warm palette. Silver on warm frames reads mismatched.

Option C

Five criteria, ten rules.

Each card lifts the implicit "don't" out of the prose and surfaces it as a strike-through. Reads as a checklist, gives the page editorial opinion, useful if the goal is to make the formula feel earned, not arbitrary.

Option D

A hero criterion, four supporting.

Magazine spread layout. One criterion gets blown up as the visual anchor (here, "Soft cat-eye", the shape that does the most work), the rest stay tight in a right column. Slightly more opinionated, very editorial.

ii. The shape

Soft cat-eye.

A subtle upsweep at the temple, just enough to lift the face. Squared cat-eye works too. Pure rounds flatten, sharp angles harden, this is the in-between.

i.
Warm frame

Tortoise, champagne, milky pink, cream. Never cool whites or greys.

iii.
Medium-large

Generous lens, restrained outline. Not tiny, not oversized.

iv.
Warm lens tint

Brown, amber, honey gradient. Makes brown eyes glow.

v.
Gold accents

Gold hardware matches the palette. Silver clashes.
